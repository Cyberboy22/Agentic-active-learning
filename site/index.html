What this calculation does and does not do

The ligand is rigid. Its conformation is fixed at whatever was generated for it, so torsional flexibility — most of what makes docking hard — is not searched. The receptor is rigid too, and desolvation and electrostatics are absent.

It can be checked. On ABL1, “Redock crystal ligand” removes imatinib from its known position and asks the engine to find it again. Across ten seeds offline it recovered the crystallographic pose to about 0.3 Å in eight runs and fell into a secondary pocket in two. You see whichever you get.

Pocket detection is crude. For structures fetched live, the search box is centred on the largest non-solvent heteroatom group in the file — usually the co-crystallised ligand, but not guaranteed. If a structure has no bound ligand there is nothing to centre on and that target is unavailable.

A rule-based stand-in for expert triage: RDKit's PAINS substructure alerts plus property limits. Every veto is logged, and vetoed compounds are not proposed again.

What is real and what is modelled. The molecules are real: published ligand sets for EGFR, CDK2 and c-Met screened against NCI library compounds, with fingerprint similarity computed as it would be in practice. Protein structures and crystallographic ligand poses are real. The affinity values driving the campaign are not measured — apart from 24 c-Met ligands carrying experimental free energies, they come from a documented structural-and-property model standing in for a docking engine, so that decision rules can be compared offline. No number here is a prediction about any compound.
