The engine samples rigid placements of the ligand on precomputed interaction grids and scores them with Vina-style terms. Scores are in those terms' arbitrary units — not kcal/mol, and not affinity predictions.

What this calculation does and does not do

It is a real search. Two gaussian attraction terms, a repulsion term, a hydrophobic term and a hydrogen-bond term, evaluated over surface distances on grids precomputed from the receptor, searched by a coarse scan over cavity space followed by annealing and local descent.

The ligand is rigid. Its internal conformation is fixed at whatever was generated for it. Torsional flexibility is most of what makes docking hard, and it is not searched here. Receptor flexibility, desolvation and electrostatics are also absent.

It can be checked. “Redock crystal ligand” removes imatinib from its known position and asks the engine to find it again. Across ten seeds offline it recovered the crystallographic pose to about 0.3 Å in eight runs and fell into a secondary pocket in two. You will see whichever you get, including the failures.

The protein is not the campaign's target. The campaigns screen against EGFR, CDK2 and c-Met; the embedded structure is ABL1, because it is a kinase with a validated docking set-up that could be bundled here. All four are protein kinases with similar ATP sites, so placing these compounds is chemically meaningful — but a pose here is not a prediction about the campaign's actual target.

A rule-based stand-in for expert triage: RDKit's PAINS substructure alerts plus property limits. Every veto is logged, and vetoed compounds are not proposed again.

What is real and what is modelled. The molecules are real: published ligand sets for EGFR, CDK2 and c-Met, screened against NCI library compounds, with fingerprint similarity computed as it would be in practice. The protein structure and the crystallographic ligand pose are real. The affinity values driving the campaign are not measured — apart from 24 c-Met ligands carrying experimental free energies, they come from a documented structural-and-property model standing in for a docking engine, so that decision rules can be compared offline. No number here is a prediction about any compound.
